Strategic Partnership Proposal for Senior Leadership

Purpose: Svashasan Labs proposes a RISC-V automotive compute and vehicle OS partnership in which InCore leads silicon and hardware economics, while Svashasan retains ownership of Svashasan OS. This briefing covers the essentials in 30 minutes.

2. The Proposal

We propose a:

Strategic RISC-V Automotive Compute & Vehicle OS Partnership

InCore brings: RISC-V processor IP, configurable cores, custom SoC capability, accelerators, security, FPGA/ASIC design flow, SDK/BSP and hardware engineering.

Svashasan brings: Svashasan OS, perception orchestration, sensor fusion, localization, ADAS, autonomy orchestration, OTA, diagnostics, fleet/cloud and OEM-facing vehicle software.

InCore becomes the customizable compute foundation; Svashasan becomes the vehicle intelligence and operating-system layer.

Our shared objective is not merely to run Svashasan OS on InCore hardware. It is to co-design an automotive compute platform in which InCore silicon is optimized around Svashasan workloads.

3. Why InCore

InCore's configurable RISC-V and custom-SoC approach gives us an opportunity to move from ordinary integration to genuine hardware–software co-design.

Instead of:

"Please make your chip compatible with Svashasan OS."

The relationship can become:

"Here are the compute, memory, accelerator, security, safety and sensor requirements generated by our vehicle software. Let's create the compute architecture around those workloads."

This creates a stronger moat for both companies.

6. Structure and Preferred Status

We suggest not starting with a 50:50 JV. Instead, we propose:

  • Phase 1: MOU / Strategic Partnership
  • Phase 2: Joint Development Agreement
  • Phase 3: Commercialization Agreement
  • Phase 4: Potential deeper alliance after a successful OEM program
InCore becomes Svashasan's preferred RISC-V silicon/IP partner for designated automotive programs.

Proposed term: 3 years + 2-year renewal. Exclusivity would be narrow and limited to specific automotive programs. Svashasan OS stays portable across platforms through a hardware abstraction layer, because OEMs may mandate other silicon.

7. Roadmap: Prove First, Customize Second

We suggest the first phase should not begin with a custom chip. We begin on an existing InCore platform, benchmark real vehicle workloads, and customize only on evidence. This substantially reduces technical and financial risk.

9. Target Customers and Use Cases

We propose prioritizing:

  • Indian EV OEMs
  • Commercial EV manufacturers
  • Autonomous shuttle and mobility platforms
  • Fleet operators
  • Robotics and industrial mobility

We suggest starting with focused use cases rather than full autonomy:

  • ADAS compute: emergency braking, forward collision warning, lane detection, adaptive cruise
  • Fleet intelligence: telemetry, diagnostics, OTA
  • Vision compute: object detection, occupancy, driver monitoring
  • Sensor fusion: camera and radar

These are easier to demonstrate and commercialize than promising complete autonomy immediately.

These are proposed starting points for discussion, to be refined jointly into final contractual terms.

Why InCore receives more

InCore carries processor IP responsibility, hardware implementation, verification, tape-out risk and manufacturing coordination. We therefore propose that InCore receives the larger share of joint hardware and platform economics, while Svashasan retains the economics of the software it independently owns.

Per-vehicle illustration

If the platform generates ₹50,000 per vehicle: InCore ₹27,500, Svashasan ₹22,500. If software and cloud generate ₹5,000 annually: Svashasan ₹4,000, InCore ₹1,000.

11. IP and Account Principles

Each party retains its core IP

  • InCore: RISC-V cores, SoC Generator, accelerators, interconnect, security IP, verification infrastructure, SDK
  • Svashasan: Svashasan OS, perception and fusion algorithms, vehicle APIs, OTA architecture, fleet software, patents

Foreground IP

  • Hardware-generated IP is InCore-owned
  • Software-generated IP is Svashasan-owned
  • Joint hardware/software technology is explicitly identified and licensed according to contribution
Joint ownership is not automatic.

Accounts and protections

The originating party leads each OEM account; jointly originated accounts are joint. Mutual non-circumvention covers introductions, technology, pricing, roadmaps and customer requirements.

12. Governance, Security and Safety

We propose a Joint Strategic Committee of 3 InCore and 3 Svashasan members, covering roadmap, OEM opportunities, architecture, IP, commercial terms and milestones.

  • Hardware Working Group: InCore-led
  • OS / Vehicle Software Working Group: Svashasan-led
  • System Integration Working Group: Joint
  • OEM / Commercial Working Group: Joint

Security is jointly defined from hardware root of trust through secure boot, trusted OS boot, encrypted vehicle communication, OTA authentication and rollback protection to fleet key management. Functional safety is a dedicated workstream, and neither party will market the combined platform as production automotive-safe until supporting evidence exists.

We would welcome InCore's agreement on the following:

  • Proceed to an MOU on the framework outlined today
  • Nominate technical and commercial leads
  • Schedule a joint technical workshop within 30 days
  • Confirm the InCore platform for Svashasan OS bring-up

InCore provides the customizable RISC-V compute foundation, Svashasan provides the vehicle operating and intelligence layer, and together they build an OEM-ready software-defined vehicle platform, with InCore receiving slightly higher economics while Svashasan retains its core OS and software IP.
